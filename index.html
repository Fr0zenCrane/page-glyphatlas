Overview

Scene text synthesis requires stroke-accurate characters in a specified layout and visual coherence with the surrounding scene. Existing glyph-image conditioning ties each glyph’s resolution to its target region, limiting fine-grained stroke guidance for small, dense or structurally complex text. GlyphAtlas retains the glyph image as a spatial anchor and supplies detailed character or word references through a dedicated glyph atlas, whose cell resolution is independent of target-region size. Atlas-anchored attention lets each text region retrieve detail from its corresponding atlas cells to refine the coarse glyph structure provided by the glyph image. This coarse-to-fine conditioning preserves explicit layout correspondence and supports accurate scene text generation and editing, including zero-shot transfer to scripts unseen during task-specific training. Random resolution augmentation further enables adjustable atlas budgets at inference.

Method

Framework

Built on FLUX.1-Fill-dev, GlyphAtlas retains the scene-aligned glyph image for layout and coarse glyph structure, and introduces a glyph atlas for fine-grained stroke guidance. Each atlas cell contains a character or word rendered at a resolution independent of its target region. The backbone’s frozen VAE encodes these detailed stroke references, which are appended to the transformer’s token sequence alongside prompt, scene and glyph-image tokens.

Atlas-anchored attention binds the atlas to the scene layout. Scene and glyph-image tokens retrieve stroke detail from the atlas cells assigned to the regions they overlap, while each cell attends to the prompt and its own region. This coarse-to-fine conditioning refines both the scene features and their glyph condition while preserving regional correspondence. Mask-weighted flow matching and a timestep-weighted text perceptual loss jointly supervise text accuracy and scene consistency, while random resolution augmentation makes the atlas token budget adjustable at inference.

Atlas Construction

Each target string is first split into an ordered sequence of text units: individual characters for scripts such as Chinese and Korean, and whole words for Latin and Arabic to preserve word structure and joining forms. Each unit is then rendered in a uniform font into its own cell, square for a character and of natural width for a word. The cell height is set independently of the target region, so even a small scene glyph receives a detailed stroke reference.

Finally, the frozen VAE encodes each cell, and patchification converts its latent into atlas tokens. The tokens are projected to the model width and arranged on a separate positional canvas, with one row per region and cells in reading order. Atlas-anchored attention establishes their correspondence with the scene. Adjusting cell height changes both the detail of the reference and its token cost.

Results · 02

Zero-Shot Transfer Across Scripts

GlyphAtlas-Bi is trained on a Chinese–English subset and evaluated on Japanese, Korean and Arabic text editing. At inference, the glyph atlas supplies detailed stroke references for the target script, which the VAE encodes and atlas-anchored attention associates with the corresponding scene regions. The same model achieves the highest ACC among the compared methods without language-specific fine-tuning across all three scripts.

Results · 03

Qualitative Comparison Results

We compare GlyphAtlas with AnyText2, TextFlux and FLUX-Text on AnyText-benchmark, TextOCR, Total-Text and ReCTS. GlyphAtlas renders the target text accurately across stylized lettering, engraved watch numerals, curved signs and small Chinese shop text, while preserving the appearance of the text and its surroundings.

Results · 04

Component Ablations and Atlas Budgeting

Glyph References and Atlas-Anchored Attention

The glyph image supplies spatial structure; the atlas adds stroke detail. Together with atlas-anchored attention, they improve small-text accuracy from 17.65% to 27.21% and Korean accuracy from 37.90% to 66.96% over the glyph-image-only model under the same training recipe.

Atlas Budgeting

Randomized cell heights make the model work across atlas resolutions. A square character cell uses 16 tokens at 64 pixels and 9 tokens at 48 pixels, a 43.75% reduction. The allocator instead assigns more detail to small scene glyphs while managing the total sequence length.
